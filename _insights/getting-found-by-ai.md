Engineers and buyers are changing how they search. Instead of typing a few keywords and opening ten tabs, many now ask an AI assistant a full question: "Which manufacturers make stainless steel multistage pumps for 40 m³/h at 120 m head?" or "Who supplies dealer portals for pump companies in India?"

The answers come from information the assistant can find and understand. For industrial manufacturers, that raises a simple question: when someone asks about your kind of product, does the answer include you?

## How AI assistants find product information

AI search tools, such as those in Google, Bing, ChatGPT and Perplexity, combine a language model with a search index. When they answer a question, they look for pages that clearly state facts relevant to it, and they favour sources that are consistent, specific and easy to interpret.

That means the same things that help traditional search help here, with more weight on clarity:

- **Clear facts stated plainly.** Who you are, what you make, for which industries, in which markets.
- **Specific product information.** Ranges, capacities, materials, standards and applications, written as text and tables, not locked inside images or PDFs.
- **Consistency.** The same facts on your website, your data sheets, your directory listings and your profiles.
- **Structured data.** Machine-readable descriptions (schema.org markup) of your organisation, products, articles and frequently asked questions.

## Why structured product data matters

Most manufacturers already have the information buyers ask about. It is just not in a form machines can read. Specifications sit in PDFs, performance data sits in curve images, and product ranges are described differently on every page.

When product data is structured (one record per product, with consistent attributes and units), it can be published as product pages, tables, data sheets and structured data from the same source. Every channel says the same thing, in a form both people and machines can use.

That is the same work that makes your catalogue, dealer portal and documentation better. Clean product data is not only internal hygiene; it is how your products become findable.

## Practical steps

1. **Publish your key facts as text.** A clear page for each product range, service and industry you serve, with the specifications that matter in the first paragraphs.
2. **Answer real questions.** Add short question-and-answer sections using the words your customers use.
3. **Get PDFs out of the way.** Keep PDFs as downloads, but put the important information on the page itself.
4. **Add structured data.** Describe your organisation, products and FAQs with schema.org markup.
5. **Be consistent everywhere.** Use the same company name, address, product names and specifications on your website, LinkedIn, directories and dealer sites.
6. **Keep it current.** Out-of-date information is worse than none. A single product database that feeds every channel keeps everything aligned.

## Where to start

Pick the product range you most want to be recommended for, and check what an AI assistant says when you ask about it. If your company is missing or described wrongly, look at what information is available about that range on your site, and whether a machine could read it.
